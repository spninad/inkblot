---
title: "Geometry of Latent World Models: Curvature & Invariance"
date: "October 2026"
abstract: |
  We examine how non-Euclidean representation geometry affects long-horizon rollout accuracy
  in learned world models. Using discrete Ricci curvature estimators, we show that regions of
  negative sectional curvature correspond to error explosion in autoregressive dynamics.
  Regularizing the latent metric tensor stabilizes multi-step rollouts without expanding model capacity.
---

<Section number="1" title="Curvature in Learned Latent Spaces">

In joint-embedding world models, an encoder $E_\theta: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Z}$ maps high-dimensional sensory observations into a continuous latent space $\mathcal{Z}$. The dynamics model $T_\phi: \mathcal{Z} \times \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{Z}$ predicts future states:

<Equation
  math="\mathbf{z}_{t+1} = T_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{a}_t) = \mathbf{z}_t + \int_0^1 \mathbf{v}_\phi(\gamma(s))\,\mathrm{d}s"
  display={true}
  caption="Continuous latent transition parameterized as integral flow."
/>

To assess geometric stability, we measure the Ollivier-Ricci curvature $\kappa(x, y)$ between neighboring latent states:

<Equation
  math="\kappa(x, y) = 1 - \frac{W_1\left(m_x, m_y\right)}{d(x, y)}"
  display={true}
  caption="Ollivier-Ricci curvature via Wasserstein distance between local probability measures."
/>

When $\kappa(x, y) < 0$, latent geodesics diverge exponentially, producing catastrophic compounding error over long rollout horizons.

</Section>
