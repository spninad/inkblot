  We present an in-depth empirical and theoretical investigation of score-based diffusion
  planning over Riemannian manifolds. By warping the score field with dynamic obstacle
  metric tensors, our planner guarantees collision avoidance and torque feasibility without
  relying on external trajectory optimization post-processing.

<Section number="1" title="Problem Formulation & Manifold Geometry">

Trajectory synthesis in continuous physical domains requires navigating complex configuration spaces $\mathcal{Q}$ with non-convex obstacle boundaries $\partial \mathcal{O}$. Standard Euclidean score matching models the distribution over state-action sequences:

<Equation
  math="\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\tau}_t = \left[ \mathbf{f}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_t, t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}} \log p_t(\boldsymbol{\tau}_t) \right] \mathrm{d}t + g(t)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathbf{w}}_t"
  display={true}
  caption="Reverse-time trajectory generation SDE."
/>

When projected through robot kinematics, Euclidean drift ignores metric curvature. We endow the configuration manifold with a Riemannian metric tensor $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{q}) = \mathbf{I} + \alpha\,\exp\left(-\frac{\mathrm{SDF}(\mathbf{q})^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^\top$.

<Aside type="note" title="Geometric Invariance">
Because the metric tensor expands eigenvalues along the surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, gradient flow under $\mathbf{G}^{-1} \nabla \mathcal{E}$ naturally deflects trajectories along tangent bundles parallel to obstacle walls.
</Aside>

</Section>
